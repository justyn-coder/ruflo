## CLAIM 1: "FBA data shows 40-50% of utility permits get rejected first pass"

**Verified: NO**

Searched FBA's `Local Permitting for Fiber Network Projects: The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly` (Sept 2025 whitepaper) and the `Fiber Deployment Cost Annual Report 2025` (FBA/Cartesian, Jan 2026). Neither contains any first-pass rejection or denial percentage. The whitepaper discusses rework, ROW disputes, and pole-attachment delays qualitatively, never quantitatively as a rejection rate.

The closest numeric FBA fact is in Cartesian §3 (p.27): "Delays in deployment timelines impact nearly 50% of respondents… nearly half (46%) of the respondents faced material project delays." That measures **project delay incidence**, not **first-pass permit rejection**.

The only "40-50%" range in the FBA whitepaper is a forward-looking efficiency claim: "Speeds up permitting by 30–50%" — referring to the savings *from* digital permitting systems, not the rejection rate.

Retrieval: 2026-06-09. Sources: `fiberbroadband.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/FBA-Whitepaper_Local-Permitting-for-Fiber-Network-Projects_FINAL-1.pdf` and `fiberbroadband.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/FBA_Cartesian_Fiber-Deployment-Cost-Annual-Report_2025.pdf`.

## CLAIM 2: "FBA flagged permit-cycle delay as the top reason BEAD timelines slip"

**Verified: PARTIAL — wording is overstated**

FBA does flag permitting as **a** top driver, not **the** top reason. From Cartesian Report 2025, p.27 verbatim:

> "Permitting, utility locates, and make-ready work were the most cited drivers of delays. Permitting issues were particularly impactful, with respondents reporting timelines extending by approximately 20% compared to previous years—extending projects by two months in many cases, and up to 18 months in the most egregious of cases."

And p.30: "Nearly two-thirds of respondents (62%) expect deployment delays… Respondents noted that permitting timelines and utility locates are expected to be key drivers of delays."

Permitting is listed **first among three** co-equal drivers and gets the longest treatment, so calling it "a top driver" is defensible. Calling it "**the** top reason BEAD timelines slip" is an overreach — FBA never ranks it #1, and the survey covers deployment delays generally, not BEAD-specific timeline slippage. FBA's BEAD-specific public statements (e.g., Gary Bolton, April 2025) attribute BEAD delays to **federal program pauses** (the Trump admin 90-day delay), not permitting.

Retrieval: 2026-06-09.

## Adversarial self-check

- **Claim 1 (NO):** Strongest counter — the FBA whitepaper has 700+ lines and the searched PDF is OCR-clean, so a 40-50% first-pass rejection stat would have surfaced via grep on "reject|denied|first.pass|first.time". It did not. There is genuinely no such FBA stat in either of the two flagship 2025/2026 reports. A weak counter: the stat could come from an FBA member-only document, a conference deck, or a podcast interview. None of those would meet our "FBA's own publications" bar even if found, and our search of FBA's public press releases and the two flagship reports came up empty.

- **Claim 2 (PARTIAL):** Counter — one could argue "most cited driver" effectively means "top reason," so the P.S. is colloquially accurate. Honest read: in the Cartesian report, permitting, utility locates, and make-ready are listed as a co-equal trio of "most cited drivers." Permitting gets the longest paragraph and the only timeline quantification, which is what makes "a top driver" defensible — but "**the** top reason" implies a ranking FBA does not publish. A second counter: FBA Gary Bolton's April 2025 BEAD statement names the federal 90-day pause as the BEAD-specific delay driver, not permitting — which actively contradicts the P.S. framing.

- **Reliance risk on Cartesian:** The Cost Annual Report is co-authored with Cartesian, a consultancy. Some readers may dispute whether it counts as "FBA's own publication." Mitigation: it is published on fiberbroadband.org, branded FBA, and FBA cites it as their data in press releases — it clears the "FBA's own publication" bar.
