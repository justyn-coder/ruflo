# JTBD Matrix — Inorsa Fiber

## The 7 Jobs

---

### JTBD 1: "Break the Permit Bottleneck" (revised per Nick McManus 2026-06-03)

**Job statement:** "When 40-50% of my permit submissions get rejected on first pass and each return costs 3-6 weeks, I want to produce drawings fast enough that my team has time for proper QC before submission, instead of rushing to meet the construction schedule."

**Who has this job:** A&E firm engineering managers, fiber operator permit coordinators, construction firm project managers. Most common persona: `permit_cycle` (17 contacts, largest bucket). Nick confirmed: "Kickback cascade is the biggest problem in the industry. Cycle time production delays are days. Kickback delays are months."

**Pain (in their words):**
- "Every permit return on an FTTX package means your team redesigns on your dime, not the client's" — Mike Rutski email to Indus CAD
- "A single permit return does not just cost rework hours. It backs up the queue behind it." — Mike Rutski email to PCCI
- 40-50% of permit submissions rejected on first pass (Nick McManus, confirmed 2026-06-03 — NOT the 8-12% we previously cited)
- Booth: "Permitting will never change" — universal sentiment from prospects

**Gain:** Drawings produced fast enough that the team can do rigorous QC BEFORE jurisdictional submission. The speed creates the time for quality. More submissions per month, better first-pass rate because they aren't rushing.

**Inorsa capability:** SPEED, not validation. Inorsa accelerates drawing production so teams have more time for their own QC. Per Nick: "We won't catch the errors, but now they have more time to." Do NOT claim Inorsa catches errors or validates inputs — errors in the GIS data translate directly to errors in the output.

**Best proof point:** "70% reduction in construction drawing cycle time" (approved). "~10 min source data to preliminary drawing" (approved). Frame as: "If your drawings take a week to produce, you rush QC. If they take 10 minutes, you QC properly."

**Best CTA:** "With 40-50% of permits rejected on first pass, how much of that is rushed QC because the drawing took too long to produce?"

**Best influence pattern:** `challenger_insight` (75% reply rate for permit_cycle personas). Lead with: the speed-creates-quality frame. Most firms think the problem is jurisdiction requirements. The real problem is they don't have time to QC properly because drawing production is too slow.

**Microsite value-add:** Workflow Map (Concept A) showing the time allocation: current (80% drafting, 20% QC) vs. with Inorsa (20% finishing, 80% QC). The shift from "rushing to produce" to "time to get it right."

**Signal to detect in research:** Prospect operates in multiple jurisdictions. Job postings mention permit coordination. High volume of submissions (more volume = more rushing = more kickbacks).

### JTBD 2: "Scale Without Hiring"

**Job statement:** "When my fiber build volume is growing faster than I can hire qualified designers, I want to multiply my team's output without adding headcount, so I can take on more work without proportional cost increases."

**Who has this job:** A&E firm principals/VPs, fiber operator engineering directors. Key persona: `build_pace` (16 contacts, second largest bucket, 6 Strong signals — highest concentration of strong fits).

**Pain (in their words):**
- "Engineers spend hours manually producing drawings from LLD and GIS inputs. Change requests stack up. Manual coordination that worked at 50 miles breaks at 250." — Chris Balandran one-pager
- "Outsourcing adds handoffs, increases risk, and leaves teams with less control over quality and timing. Hiring adds headcount without fixing the underlying problem." — Chris Balandran one-pager
- "Can't find fiber designers" — industry baseline (3:1 job-to-candidate ratio, 58,000 new roles needed by 2032)
- Substrate (Dawson/FBA study): "The industry is going to be short of the fiber construction workers, fiber technicians, and engineers needed to meet the upcoming broadband deployment driven by BEAD"
- Booth: Indus CAD — 200+ employees, offshore team, still doing manual GIS-to-CAD conversion
- Booth: Booker Engineering — Spencer Kariniemi reached out INBOUND asking about "automated basemap drafting" because his team independently identified the need

**Gain:** Significant increase in drafting capacity with existing headcount (2-5x range per sales deck, but actual automation potential depends on how well they leverage their network management tool — per Nick, do NOT promise a specific percentage without file review). Bid on larger projects without hiring.

**Inorsa capability:** Drawing Agent — automates 80% of the drafting lift. "We automate roughly 80% of the drafting lift. Your team does the last mile." (sales deck p6)

**Best proof point:** "2-5x drafting scaling capacity" and "~10 min source data to preliminary drawing" (B+T deck, pending Chris approval). "56 hours to under 3 minutes" (customer thread, pending approval).

**Best CTA:** "How many linear feet can your current team handle per month, and what happens when BEAD pushes that number up 3x?"

**Best influence pattern:** `challenger_insight` (75% for build_pace personas) or `social_proof` (50%, small N). Lead with: "Firms at your scale with 2-5x more volume are doing it with FEWER drafters by automating the GIS-to-CAD step."

**Microsite value-add:** ROI Calculator (Concept B) — prospect enters their team size and LF/month, sees capacity gained without hiring. OR Peer Benchmark (Concept D) showing where they sit vs. 71 firms analyzed.

**Signal to detect in research:** Company posting job ads for CAD designers or fiber engineers. Company mentions offshore teams. Company recently won BEAD awards or large contracts. Growth signals in research (acquisitions, new markets, headcount growth).

### JTBD 3: "Make BEAD Economics Work"

**Job statement:** "When I've won a BEAD award with fixed funding rates, I want predictable engineering costs per linear foot, so I can build profitably at 20-30 cents/LF without margin erosion."

**Who has this job:** Fiber operator CFOs/finance directors, A&E firm principals bidding BEAD work, BEAD sub-grantee program managers. Key personas: `capital_efficiency` (2 contacts, both Strong), `program_leverage` (8 contacts).

**Pain (in their words):**
- "Our CEO/CFO have come to the unfortunate conclusion that the cost is too high and will have a direct impact on EBITDA since we can't capitalize any of it" — Customer VP of Engineering (real objection from sales thread)
- "Profitability at BEAD-funded rates (20 to 30 cents per linear foot) is the core objective" — Nathan Dunn call recap to B+T
- Substrate (Dawson): "88% of respondents expect costs to increase again in 2026" — Rising Costs of Broadband Construction
- Substrate (Dawson): "The industry is focused on BEAD grants... a lot of federal broadband grants facing completion deadlines" — Broadband Grant Deadlines
- Substrate (Dawson): Congress pushing permitting reform specifically because BEAD timelines are at risk

**Gain:** Predictable, per-LF engineering cost that fits within BEAD-funded rates. Token-based pricing that scales with volume — no large upfront commitment, per-unit cost drops at scale. OPEX, not CAPEX.

**Inorsa capability:** Token-based SaaS pricing scaled to linear feet. No seat licenses. Pay-as-you-go — tokens consumed as drawings are generated. One-time professional services for implementation separate from subscription. NOTE: Do NOT promise CAPEX treatment or professional services restructuring — that was a one-off negotiation, not a standard offering. The pitch is: "Yes it's OPEX. The throughput gain more than covers it."

**Best proof point:** "On longer-route fiber projects, a week of manual drafting can compress to minutes of automation plus your team's finishing work" (B+T deck p7). "~10 min source data to preliminary drawing" (deck, approved). Cartesian cost benchmarks: $18/ft underground, 72% labor — Inorsa reduces the labor component.

**Best CTA:** "What's your current engineering cost per linear foot, and how does that change when BEAD volume hits?"

**Best influence pattern:** `loss_aversion` — but ONLY for capital_efficiency personas (only segment where it got a reply). Lead with the financial frame, not the engineering frame.

**Microsite value-add:** BEAD Timeline Pressure Tool (Concept C) — shows their state's BEAD construction deadlines vs. their estimated drawing production pace. Plus ROI framing: "At $18/ft underground with 72% labor, even a 2x throughput improvement changes the unit economics."

**Signal to detect in research:** Company is a named BEAD sub-grantee (NTIA portals). Company mentions BEAD in job postings or press releases. Company is PE-owned (EBITDA sensitivity). Company operating in states with aggressive BEAD timelines.

### JTBD 4: "Standardize Across Markets"

**Job statement:** "When I'm submitting drawings to dozens of different jurisdictions with different standards, I want one consistent process that adapts to each jurisdiction's requirements, so I can stop reformatting the same design data for every county."

**Who has this job:** Multi-state A&E firms, national fiber operators, large contractors. Key persona: `permit_cycle` and `build_pace` overlap. This job was explicitly stated by multiple prospects.

**Pain (in their words):**
- "Every US municipality still has its own permit requirements" — from IMMCO and Centillion ShowRev emails
- "LLDs and permit inputs vary across teams or markets, even for similar builds" — Inorsa sales deck p8, discovery question 4
- "GIS designs move fast, but converting them to construction-grade AutoCAD remains manual, slow, and different for each client's drawing standard" — Nathan Dunn email to Cyient
- Booth: Jackie (Dallas firm, 250 active fiber projects) — "Multiple design vendors submit different drawing standards. Wants single platform to standardize."
- Brain synthesis: "Multi-state operators — each state = different DOT, different standards" — B+T GRP (50 states), Rayco (5 states)

**Gain:** AutoCAD output to each jurisdiction's standard from a single input process. "Standardize deliverables across multiple client specs at once" (Nathan Dunn email to Cyient).

**Inorsa capability:** Generate step — "AutoCAD package to YOUR standard, plus PDF for permitting" (sales deck p6). Drawing Agent "maps to your CAD blocks, generates pages with cross-references and jurisdictional callouts."

**Best proof point:** "Automate GIS-to-CAD conversion, validate cross-document inputs before production begins, and standardize deliverables across multiple client specs at once" (Nathan Dunn, Cyient email — best problem/solution statement).

**Best CTA:** "Do your LLDs and permit inputs vary across teams or markets, even for similar builds?" (Inorsa deck slide 8, question 4)

**Best influence pattern:** `challenger_insight`. Lead with the standardization gap.

**Microsite value-add:** Workflow Map (Concept A) showing multiple jurisdictions converging through one Drawing Agent into jurisdiction-specific outputs. Visual impact: 5 jurisdictions, 5 different standards, 1 process.

**Signal to detect in research:** Company operates in 3+ states. Company has multiple offices. Company mentions "multi-state" or "national" in description. Company has offshore teams (implies multiple delivery locations needing standard output).

### JTBD 5: "Keep Crews Moving"

**Job statement:** "When my construction crews are idle because engineering drawings aren't ready, I want drawings to stay ahead of the construction schedule, so I can stop paying for crew downtime caused by the documentation layer."

**Who has this job:** Construction contractors, fiber operator construction managers. Key persona: `build_pace` (contractor sub-segment).

**Pain (in their words):**
- "The bottleneck is not crews or equipment. It is whether CAD-ready drawings stay ahead of the construction schedule." — Lucas Spencer email to Ohio Gig
- Brain synthesis: "Crews and equipment idle when permits are delayed" — Lighthouse, Rayco pain points
- Brain synthesis: "Speed of engineering pipeline governing construction schedule" — universal contractor pain
- "When a drawing set comes back in one region, field crews in that region sit idle. Multiply that across four simultaneous builds" — Lucas Spencer email to Ohio Gig

**Gain:** Engineering drawings are never the bottleneck. Construction schedule drives the timeline, not the documentation queue.

**Inorsa capability:** Speed — "~10 min source data to preliminary drawing" (B+T deck). "Fiber activations in days, not months." The automation makes drawing production essentially instantaneous relative to the construction timeline.

**Best proof point:** "~10 min source data to preliminary drawing" (pending approval). "A week of manual drafting can compress to minutes of automation plus your team's finishing work" (B+T deck p7).

**Best CTA:** "How many crew-days per quarter are lost waiting on engineering deliverables?"

**Best influence pattern:** `loss_aversion` — for contractors who feel the daily cost of idle crews. Frame around financial loss per idle day.

**Microsite value-add:** BEAD Timeline tool adapted for contractors: "Your awarded projects × your current drawing pace = X weeks of crew idle time. With Inorsa: 0."

**Signal to detect in research:** Company is a construction/installation firm. Company mentions "field crews" or "construction schedule" in job postings. Company has BEAD construction contracts with fixed timelines.

### JTBD 6: "Deliver Faster, Protect the Relationship" (revised per Nick McManus 2026-06-03)

**Job statement:** "When my A&E firm is juggling multiple ISP clients with different deadlines, I want to deliver drawings faster so my clients see us as reliable and responsive, not as the bottleneck in their construction schedule."

**Who has this job:** A&E firm account managers, engineering firm principals. Key persona: `drawings_quality` (3 contacts, underserved by current outreach).

**Pain (in their words):**
- "When an input conflict in the source data makes it through drafting and into a permit submission, the rejection comes back to your firm. Your team redesigns. Your client questions the QC process." — Mike Rutski email to Indus CAD (Kesari)
- Brain synthesis: "Client retention risk when permits come back rejected" — A&E segment pain #5
- Per Nick: A&E delays become operator delays. The motivation is money — acceleration of paypoints (cycle time) and more paypoints per month (volume).

**Gain:** Faster turnaround to clients. More capacity to serve multiple clients simultaneously. Reliability as a differentiator.

**Inorsa capability:** SPEED — accelerated drawing production means faster delivery to the ISP client. Per Nick: Inorsa does NOT catch errors in the GIS data. But faster production gives the A&E team more time for their own QC before delivering to the client. The result is faster AND better-reviewed output.

**Best proof point:** "70% reduction in construction drawing cycle time" (approved). "~10 min source data to preliminary drawing" (approved). Frame as: deliver in days what used to take weeks.

**Best CTA:** "How fast does your client expect turnaround on a drawing package, and how close are you to that today?"

**Best influence pattern:** `social_proof`. Lead with: "A&E firms at your scale are compressing delivery timelines from weeks to days."

**Microsite value-add:** Peer Benchmark (Concept D) showing delivery timelines: industry average vs. firms using automation. Focus on speed metrics, NOT error rates (we can't claim error reduction).

**Signal to detect in research:** Company is an A&E firm serving ISP clients. Company has multiple active clients. Company mentions turnaround time or delivery speed. Company has offshore teams (implies coordination overhead that slows delivery).

### JTBD 7: "Win the BEAD Bid"

**Job statement:** "When I'm competing for BEAD sub-grantee work and need to demonstrate engineering capacity, I want to show I can handle the volume without the risk, so I can win the contract over firms that look capacity-constrained."

**Who has this job:** A&E firms and contractors bidding on BEAD-funded projects. Key persona: `program_leverage` (8 contacts).

**Pain (in their words):**
- Substrate (Dawson): "The industry is going to be short of the fiber construction workers, fiber technicians, and engineers needed to meet the upcoming broadband deployment" — workforce shortage is a KNOWN risk factor for BEAD awards
- Brain synthesis: BEAD timeline pressure affecting Avatar Tech, Ohio Gig, TEC
- "Looking for tier one operator contracts and other customers" — Indus CAD at booth (signaling they want to grow)
- Spencer Kariniemi (Booker) reached out INBOUND: "my team wanted more info on automated basemap drafting" — they were actively looking for capacity solutions

**Gain:** Credible bid demonstrating engineering capacity. Win contracts that would otherwise go to larger firms.

**Inorsa capability:** Capacity multiplier — significant drafting scaling capacity without hiring. Per Nick: "It is reasonable to say that the increased capacity over doing the task manually provides confidence in the bid and smooths the instability of sporadic work." Do NOT promise "80%" without file review.

**Best proof point:** "2-5x drafting scaling capacity" (B+T deck). Backed by engagement model showing fast onboarding (Introduction → Demo → Workshop → Proposal, then "your team using it on real work").

**Best CTA:** "Are you bidding on BEAD work that would stretch your current engineering capacity?"

**Best influence pattern:** `challenger_insight`. Lead with: "The firms winning BEAD contracts aren't the biggest — they're the ones that can demonstrate capacity at scale without the headcount risk."

**Microsite value-add:** BEAD Timeline tool showing their state's award timeline + a capacity calculator. "At your current team size, you can handle X LF/year. BEAD requires Y. Inorsa bridges the gap."

**Signal to detect in research:** Company is in a BEAD-active state. Company is mid-size (50-300 employees) competing against larger firms. Company has recent BEAD-related job postings or press releases. Company mentions "capacity" or "scale" in any context.

## How This Matrix Drives the System

### Research agents
Detect which JTBD applies to each prospect. Look for the "Signal to detect" column. Tag the prospect with 1-2 primary JTBDs.

### Composer
Use the "Best CTA" for each JTBD as the email closing question. Use "Pain (in their words)" for the opening framing. Use "Best influence pattern" to guide Thompson Sampling.

### Pattern selector (Thompson Sampling)
Weight by JTBD-persona combination, not just persona alone. `challenger_insight` dominates JTBDs 1, 2, 4, 7. `loss_aversion` applies ONLY to JTBD 3 and 5. `social_proof` for JTBD 6.

### ABM microsite
Map to the "Microsite value-add" for the prospect's primary JTBD. T1 = Workflow Map (universal). T2 = JTBD-specific tool (calculator, benchmark, or timeline). T3 = demo path.

### Prioritizer (P2 cold list)
Score each cold prospect by how many JTBD signals they match. More signals = higher research priority.
