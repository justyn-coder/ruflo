### What they do

Dobson Fiber is a vertically integrated fiber-optic ISP providing residential gigabit internet (up to 10 Gbps, 100 Gbps roadmap within 5 years), business broadband, and wholesale services across Oklahoma, Arkansas, and Texas. Founded by Everett Dobson — who previously built Dobson Communications Corporation and sold it to AT&T for $5.1B in 2007 — the current entity is executing an aggressive fiber buildout backed by $700M in deployment capital from iCON Infrastructure Partners V (London-based infrastructure PE firm, $1.9B fund).

### Geographic footprint

- **Oklahoma City MSA**: OKC, Yukon, Edmond, Midwest City, Del City, Norman
- **Tulsa MSA**: Tulsa, Broken Arrow (100K+ pop, groundbreaking Dec 2024), Owasso, Sapulpa, Jenks
- **Rural Oklahoma**: 52+ communities including Altus, Enid, Lawton, McAlester, Duncan, Clinton, Elk City, Weatherford, Ada, Okmulgee, Madill
- **Arkansas**: 5 communities
- **Texas**: Mentioned in deployment plan, specifics not public
- Q1 2025 construction launched in: Okmulgee, Madill, Oakland, Haskell, Taft, Burns Flat

### Recent news & growth signals

1. **$700M deployment plan** backed by iCON Infrastructure Partners V (closed ~Q3 2022). Largest private fiber deployment in Oklahoma history.
2. **BEAD-funded projects**: $11.6M federal grants via Oklahoma Broadband Office + $5.7M Dobson matching funds = 1,829 homes/businesses. Two projects in Washita and Greer counties completed Jan 2026 (366 locations). Remaining projects expected complete Q2 2026.
3. **Ciena 6500 RLS deployment** — upgraded backbone infrastructure for 10G-to-100G capacity.
4. **Broken Arrow groundbreaking** (Dec 2024) — multimillion-dollar expansion into Tulsa metro's second-largest city.
5. **Active hiring**: 17 open positions on Glassdoor, 26 on ZipRecruiter. Key roles: OSP Construction Manager, OSP Engineer, OSP Design Engineer I, Assistant Director of OSP Engineering, NOC Engineer, Installation & Repair Technician.
6. **Expansion pace**: From 35 communities (mid-2024) to 38+ (early 2025), with 6 new town launches in Q1 2025.

### Technology stack (inferred)

- **Network**: Ciena 6500 RLS backbone, GPON FTTH, 10G-capable (100G roadmap)
- **OSP Design**: Hiring OSP Design Engineers and OSP Construction Managers who "coordinate with OSP Planning to prepare cost estimates and route proposals" — implies in-house OSP design team. Specific CAD/GIS tools not publicly disclosed, but standard for Oklahoma fiber operators: likely AutoCAD + ArcGIS or 3-GIS.
- **Construction**: Mix of in-house and contractor crews across geographic zones.

### Key projects & BEAD involvement

- Oklahoma Broadband Office partnership: $17.3M total investment (federal + matching) connecting 1,829 locations
- Washita County and Greer County projects — first completed BEAD-funded projects in Oklahoma (Jan 2026)
- Oklahoma Broadband Governing Board grant recipient
- Remaining BEAD projects to finalize Q2 2026

### Challenges & pain points (inferred from signals)

1. **Scale vs. staffing**: 246 employees deploying across 38+ communities with a $700M capital plan. The gap between capital available and design/construction throughput is the defining constraint. Hiring 17+ roles simultaneously — including OSP engineers and construction managers — confirms capacity is the bottleneck, not funding.
2. **Multi-market permitting**: Expanding simultaneously into OKC MSA, Tulsa MSA, and rural Oklahoma means navigating dozens of different municipal permitting regimes, pole attachment processes, and ROW rules. Each jurisdiction = different timeline.
3. **Design-to-construction pacing**: Engineering designs reported "complete in much of the service territory" — but permitting and construction are still catching up. Classic fiber deployment bottleneck: design outruns permitting, permitting outruns construction.
4. **BEAD compliance timeline**: Remaining BEAD-funded projects due Q2 2026. Grant-funded builds have hard deadlines with reporting obligations.

## 3. ICP + JTBD Mapping

### ICP fit: STRONG

Dobson Fiber is a mid-size operator ($700M capital plan, 246 employees) executing a massive multi-market fiber buildout across three states. They are simultaneously expanding into urban metros (OKC, Tulsa) and rural Oklahoma communities with BEAD funding. The gap between capital availability and design/construction throughput is visible in their hiring patterns (OSP engineers, construction managers) and their public statements about engineering designs being complete while construction catches up.

### JTBD mapping

| JTBD | Relevance | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1: Break the permit bottleneck** | HIGH | Expanding into dozens of jurisdictions simultaneously. OKC MSA + Tulsa MSA + rural = wildly different permitting regimes. Each permit kickback = weeks of delay on a $700M buildout. |
| **2: Scale without hiring** | HIGH | 246 employees, 38+ communities, $700M deployment. Hiring OSP Design Engineers and OSP Construction Managers simultaneously. Trying to staff up but labor market is 3:1 jobs-to-candidates for fiber designers. |
| **3: Make BEAD economics work** | HIGH | $17.3M in BEAD-funded projects with hard Q2 2026 deadline. Grant builds have compliance requirements (prevailing wage, Buy America, environmental, reporting) that add cost. Design speed = margin protection. |
| **5: Keep crews moving** | HIGH | Zone-by-zone construction rollout across multiple communities. If design/permitting can't feed the construction pipeline fast enough, crews sit idle on a $700M budget. |
| **7: Win the BEAD bid** | MODERATE | Already won BEAD awards. Future rounds may apply but current focus is execution, not application. |

**Primary JTBD for outreach**: JTBD 2 (Scale without hiring) + JTBD 1 (Break the permit bottleneck)

**Challenger insight**: Dobson has $700M in capital and 38+ communities to build — but only 246 people to do it. Every OSP design engineer they can't hire is a community that stays dark longer. The question isn't whether they have money. It's whether they have enough design throughput to spend it before competitors like Kinetic capture the subscriber base.
