### What they do

Finley Engineering is a nationwide A&E consulting firm serving the broadband and electric power industries. Founded in 1953, they've spent 70+ years designing fiber and power networks for telecoms, ISPs, rural electric cooperatives, municipalities, and government agencies. They are end-to-end: feasibility studies through construction inspection.

Core capability is **OSP fiber engineering** — FTTH network design, make-ready engineering, aerial and underground routing, GIS mapping, permitting documentation, and construction oversight. They also run a major **BEAD consulting practice**, helping clients navigate the $42.5B federal broadband funding program from application through compliance.

Secondary line of business is **electric power engineering** — transmission and distribution design for utilities and cooperatives.

### Specializations

1. **OSP FTTH Engineering** — Full-cycle fiber-to-the-home design: routing, splicing, make-ready, PON architecture, construction drawings
2. **Make-ready Engineering** — Pole attachment analysis, clearance calculations, utility coordination for aerial fiber on existing power poles
3. **BEAD / Federal Funding** — Grant application preparation, feasibility studies, compliance consulting. Published whitepapers: "Don't Wait for BEAD" (advising clients to pursue parallel funding) and make-ready requirements in the BEAD era
4. **GIS / Spatial Intelligence** — Dedicated Director of Spatial Intelligence (Brian Udell). Esri GIS as industry standard. Published guidance on migrating from AutoCAD to GIS ("Outgrowing AutoCAD"). GIS-driven network planning, data transformation, color-coded mapping for funding applications
5. **Electric Power** — T&D engineering for cooperatives and utilities
6. **Construction Management** — Staking, inspection, acceptance testing, as-built documentation

### BEAD involvement

Finley is deeply embedded in the BEAD ecosystem — from both sides:

- **Advisory side**: Helps clients prepare BEAD applications, navigate compliance requirements (prevailing wage, Buy America, environmental review, reporting obligations), and structure feasibility studies that meet state broadband office standards
- **Engineering side**: Designs the fiber networks that BEAD funding builds — FTTH, make-ready, construction documentation
- **Published position**: "Don't Wait for BEAD" whitepaper advises clients to pursue established broadband funding programs in parallel — competition for BEAD is fierce (applications exceed funding by 5x in some states)
- **Timing**: BEAD construction wave expected 2026-2030. Finley's clients are entering the build phase now. Design throughput is about to become the critical constraint.

### Challenges and pain points (inferred from signals)

1. **Scale vs. throughput**: 267 employees serving clients nationwide across dozens of concurrent BEAD-funded fiber builds. Hiring PE-licensed OSP engineers (5-10 and 12+ years experience) — these are the scarcest people in fiber. Every unfilled role = a client project that moves slower.
2. **Multi-client standardization**: Each client has different GIS data quality, different CAD drawing standards, different jurisdictional requirements. Finley's engineers must context-switch across clients constantly. Standardization is margin.
3. **AutoCAD-to-GIS migration**: Their own whitepaper identifies this as a pain point for their clients — but it's also their pain. Converting legacy data and maintaining dual workflows (CAD + GIS) for different clients adds overhead.
4. **Make-ready complexity**: Pole attachment permitting involves multiple utilities per pole, different attachment rules per utility, clearance calculations, and coordination timelines that can stretch months. Finley does this manually for every pole.
5. **BEAD compliance burden**: Prevailing wage documentation, Buy America material tracking, environmental review, tribal consultation, ADA compliance, Davis-Bacon reporting — all layered on top of the engineering work. Compliance is a tax on throughput.
