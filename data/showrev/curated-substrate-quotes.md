### Quote 1
**Source:** Cartesian Fiber Deployment Cost Report 2025 — Section 19
**JTBD:** 3 (Make BEAD economics work)
**Quote:** "Engineering accounted for approximately 15% of total fiber project costs on average, with permitting at 10%. While respondents report that engineering costs contribute more to overall costs than make-ready work and permitting, most noted that permitting delays cause a domino effect on other project cost components, introducing uncertainty that further inflates planning costs and timelines."
**Why it's good:** Quantifies the cost split engineers rarely see in one place. The "domino effect" language validates the cascading delay problem.

**Source:** Cartesian Fiber Deployment Cost Report 2025 — Section 12
**JTBD:** 2 (Scale without hiring)

### Quote 16
**Source:** Dept. of Commerce Office of Inspector General, via MercuryZ BEAD labor analysis
**URL:** https://www.mercuryz.com/bead-program/bead-labor-deficit-workforce-solutions/
**JTBD:** 2 (Scale without hiring)
**Quote:** "The U.S. broadband industry needs 205,000 additional fiber technicians. Phase 1 (2025-2026) specifically demands OSP Engineers and GIS Specialists. Phase 2 (2026-2028) sees peak demand for splicers and bore operators. The workforce need peaks during the 2025-2027 window but drops significantly toward 2031."
**Why it's good:** The PHASED demand is the insight. It peaks 2025-2027 then drops — meaning permanent hires for a temporary surge is the wrong economic decision. Automation that scales up and back down is the right one.
✅ / ⚠️ / ❌ ___

### Quote 17
**Source:** FBA + PCCA Workforce Study (Continuum Capital), via Fierce Network
**URL:** https://www.fierce-network.com/broadband/us-short-about-58000-tradespeople-deploy-broadband
**JTBD:** 2 (Scale without hiring)
**Quote:** "The industry needs 58,000 new workers by 2032 — 28,000 construction jobs and 30,000 technician positions — to handle demand from BEAD and other federal programs. On top of that, 120,000 additional workers are needed to replace retirees. 20% of the current broadband workforce is over age 55."
**Why it's good:** 58K new + 120K replacements = 178K total gap. The retirement wave on top of BEAD demand makes the math impossible through hiring alone.
